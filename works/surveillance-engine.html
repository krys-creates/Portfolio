/ Surveillance Engine is...

an interactive artwork inspired by Shoshana Zuboff’s ‘surveillance capitalism’ (more about below). As you type personal information (age, email, location…), your digital portrait, drawn live from your webcam, gradually assembles out of the very words you typed.

// live artwork, best on a laptop with a webcam. It will ask for camera access. Keep sound on. open in a new tab here >

< HOW TO
USE IT >

1/ type personal details: age, location, email, last purchase… (made-up ones work too)

2/ hover over your portrait to see which data category built each part

3/ keep typing until you reach the critical exposure threshold

4/ optional: change how the portrait looks with the render style sliders

< THE SENSE
OF THE WORK >

i/ real vs synthetic

The piece stages the tension between your private, physical self and the surveilled, datafied version of it that lives in digital systems: your real keystrokes and information against the data points, categories and profiles that surveillance capitalism turns them into.

ii/ conceptual design choices

  • Empty canvas: nothing shows until you type, so disclosure decides visibility.
  • Typed text as portrait: your characters become the ASCII symbols that form your face.
  • Exposure categories: identity, location, financial, behavioural and biometric data mirror how data brokers segment people. Hover to see which one built each part of your face.
  • Escalating warnings: from “[!] DATA COLLECTION IN PROGRESS” to “[!!!] CRITICAL EXPOSURE THRESHOLD”, so self-disclosure feels done to you, not by you.
  • Sound and light: noise grows as you type and every keypress flashes, making each character a transmission.
  • Retro terminal look: CRT scanlines, phosphor glow and Matrix green nod to early computing and hacker culture.

< TECH
IMPLEMENTATION >

I built it in p5.js and plain JavaScript, with everything calculated live:

  • Keyboard: typing raises a density value (0 to 1 over 150 characters) that sets how much of the webcam image shows. It also weaves your text into the portrait, flashes the screen, fills five exposure bars, triggers warnings and scales the noise.
  • Mouse: hovering a tagged grid cell lights it in its category colour and shows a tooltip.
  • Webcam: live video is sampled 60 times a second. Each pixel’s brightness, weighted towards green and stretched for contrast, picks an ASCII character: dense for dark areas, sparse for bright. Nothing is pre-rendered.
  • Sound: smoothed noise whose volume follows your typing. Audio starts on your first click, as browsers require.
  • Glow: every character is drawn twice for the phosphor bloom, with a scanline sweep and a fading keystroke flash.
